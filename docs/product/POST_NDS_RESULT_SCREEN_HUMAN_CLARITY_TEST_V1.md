## 1. Purpose

This document defines the exact human clarity test for the new post-NDS result screen.

The purpose is to answer one product-critical question:

**After seeing this screen, does a high school student know exactly what to do next?**

This is not a broad usability study.
This is a focused clarity test for the post-direction conversion layer.

---

## 2. Why this test matters

The product finding to date is:

- direction selection is often reasonably strong,
- but post-direction output has been too abstract,
- and students may still feel: “Okay, but what do I actually write now?”

The replacement screen copy was built to fix that.

This test exists to determine whether that fix is actually working for a human reader.

---

## 3. Test question

The screen passes only if the student can answer, quickly and confidently:

1. What is my essay about?
2. What do I write first?
3. What should I avoid?
4. What do I write right after the opening?
5. Which button should I press next?

If the student cannot answer those cleanly, the screen is not clear enough yet.

---

## 4. Scope

## In scope
This test evaluates:
- wording clarity
- action clarity
- sequencing clarity
- CTA clarity
- immediate student usability

## Out of scope
This test does not evaluate:
- long-term writing quality
- overall app UX
- brand perception
- pricing
- full essay quality
- NDS direction-selection accuracy itself

This is only about:
**whether the post-NDS screen tells the student what to do next clearly enough.**

---

## 5. Test participants

## 5.1 Minimum participant count
Minimum:
- 3 participants

Preferred:
- 5 participants

This is a fast clarity test, not a large sample study.

## 5.2 Ideal participant profile
Best participant:
- current high school junior or senior
- college-applicant age range
- not deeply trained in essay coaching
- moderately stressed / normal student mindset

## 5.3 Acceptable proxies if needed
If direct student access is limited, acceptable proxies are:
- parent who has recently supported essay work
- counselor-type reviewer
- adult instructed to act as a stressed high school senior

Direct students are preferred.
But proxy testing is acceptable for early signal gathering.

---

## 6. Test materials

Use the actual post-NDS result screen.

Do not paraphrase it verbally.
Do not explain it before the participant reads it.

The participant must see:
- title
- subhead
- “why this direction works”
- “what to write first”
- “what not to do”
- “what to write right after the opening”
- focused question
- CTA buttons

Use the actual product screen if possible.
If not, use a static capture of the exact screen.

---

## 7. Test setup

## 7.1 Conditions
- participant sees the screen individually
- no coaching during first read
- no extra explanation during first response
- no hints unless follow-up phase begins

## 7.2 Timebox
Give the participant:
- 60 to 90 seconds to read the screen

Then begin questioning.

## 7.3 Interviewer rule
The interviewer must remain neutral.
Do not explain the intended meaning before the participant responds.

---

## 8. Core test script

Ask these questions in order.

### Q1
**In one sentence, what is this essay supposed to be about?**

### Q2
**What would you write first if you were starting right now?**

### Q3
**What does this screen tell you not to do?**

### Q4
**What are you supposed to write right after the opening?**

### Q5
**If you had to choose right now, which button would you press, and why?**

### Q6
**Do you feel ready to start writing after reading this, or still unsure? Why?**

These six questions are the minimum required script.

---

## 9. Scoring rubric

Score each participant response on the following dimensions.

Use:
- pass
- partial
- fail

## 9.1 Essay-about clarity
Can the participant correctly explain what the essay is really about?

### Pass
They identify the real center accurately.

### Partial
They identify part of it, but remain vague or somewhat off-center.

### Fail
They misread the essay center or cannot explain it.

## 9.2 First-step clarity
Can the participant say what they would write first?

### Pass
They can describe a concrete opening move.

### Partial
They know they should “start with a moment” but cannot explain specifically enough.

### Fail
They still do not know how to begin.

## 9.3 Anti-pattern clarity
Can the participant name what to avoid?

### Pass
They can name at least 2 major things to avoid.

### Partial
They identify one warning but not the main mistakes.

### Fail
They cannot say what the screen warns against.

## 9.4 Next-step clarity
Can the participant say what comes after the opening?

### Pass
They can describe the next writing move correctly.

### Partial
They understand there is a next move but cannot explain it clearly.

### Fail
They do not know what to write after the opening.

## 9.5 CTA clarity
Can the participant choose the right next action confidently?

### Pass
They pick a CTA and explain why clearly.

### Partial
They hesitate or choose without confidence.

### Fail
They do not know which button to press or what the buttons mean.

## 9.6 Overall readiness
Does the participant feel ready to begin writing?

### Pass
They say they could start now.

### Partial
They are closer, but still somewhat unsure.

### Fail
They still feel stuck.

---

## 10. Required observation notes

For each participant, capture:

- participant type
  - student
  - parent proxy
  - counselor/reviewer proxy
- time to first confident answer
- points of hesitation
- exact phrases they repeat back
- exact phrases that confuse them
- whether they ask for clarification
- whether they misread any CTA

These notes are as important as the pass/fail ratings.

---

## 11. Fast interpretation rules

## 11.1 Strong pass
The screen is likely working if:
- most participants can answer all core questions cleanly,
- most participants say they could start writing now,
- CTA choice is obvious,
- confusion is minor rather than structural.

## 11.2 Conditional pass
The screen is improved but still needs tightening if:
- participants understand the direction,
- but still hesitate on what to write first or next,
- or CTA choice is slightly unclear,
- or one section consistently causes confusion.

## 11.3 Fail
The screen fails if:
- participants still say “I get it, but I don’t know what to do now,”
- they cannot explain the next writing move,
- they misread the essay center,
- or the CTA logic is confusing.

---

## 12. Common failure signals to watch for

These indicate the screen still is not good enough:

- “I think I get it, but I’m not sure how to start.”
- “It sounds good, but I’d still need help.”
- “I know the idea, but not what I’d actually write.”
- “I’m not sure what the second button means.”
- “I understand the warning, but not how to avoid it.”
- “I’d probably still open ChatGPT and ask what to do next.”

Any of those signals matter.

---

## 13. What to change based on results

## If participants fail essay-about clarity
Tighten the subhead and essay-about explanation.

## If participants fail first-step clarity
Make “what to write first” more concrete and sentence-level.

## If participants fail anti-pattern clarity
Rewrite warnings in plainer language.

## If participants fail next-step clarity
Strengthen “what to write right after the opening.”

## If participants fail CTA clarity
Rename or reorder buttons.

## If participants still feel stuck overall
The whole screen is still too advisory and must become more operational.

## 15. Decision thresholds

Use these thresholds:

## Pass
Pass if:
- most participants can explain what the essay is about,
- most participants know what to write first,
- most participants know what to write next,
- most participants choose a CTA confidently,
- most participants say they could start now.

## Conditional pass
Use if:
- the screen is clearly better,
- but one or two sections still create repeated confusion.

## Fail
Fail if:
- participants still do not know how to proceed,
- they still need external clarification,
- or chat still feels clearly more useful than this screen.

---

## 16. Anti-patterns to forbid

- explaining the screen before asking questions
- coaching the participant toward the right answer
- asking leading questions
- treating polite feedback as success
- counting “this looks good” as pass evidence
- ignoring hesitation because the wording sounds smart

This test is about clarity, not polish.

---

## 17. Final standard

The post-NDS result screen is good enough only if a student can read it and begin writing immediately without needing another layer of interpretation.

That is the standard this test must measure.
