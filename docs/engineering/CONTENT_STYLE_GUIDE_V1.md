# CONTENT_STYLE_GUIDE_V1

## The College Admissions Edge

## v1 Content, Voice, and Messaging Standards

## 1. Purpose

This document defines the official content and style standard for v1 of **The College Admissions Edge**.

It exists to ensure that all user-facing language feels:

* clear
* premium
* trustworthy
* calm
* modern
* human
* consistent across the product

This guide applies to:

* landing pages
* onboarding copy
* dashboard copy
* snapshot copy
* upgrade prompts
* billing copy
* school/deadline messaging
* support language
* emails and reminders
* parent/supporting-adult surfaces
* AI-guided output wrappers and interface text

This is not a visual design guide.
This is not a grammar textbook.
This is the messaging and language system for the app.

---

## 2. Brand communication goal

The College Admissions Edge should sound like:

* a calm admissions strategist
* a strong writing coach
* a trustworthy modern product
* a disciplined guide for families

It should **not** sound like:

* a generic chatbot
* a pushy sales funnel
* a luxury-consulting cliché machine
* a robotic SaaS help desk
* a teenage tutoring app
* a corporate compliance memo

The product promise is not:
“AI writes your essays for you.”

The product promise is:
**We help students discover, shape, and communicate their authentic story with more structure, clarity, and confidence.**

---

## 3. Voice pillars

All product language should align with these five voice pillars.

## 3.1 Clear

Language should be easy to understand on the first read.
Avoid jargon, filler, and complexity for its own sake.

### Good

“Choose the direction that feels most like you.”

### Bad

“Select the narrative framework that best aligns with your authentic applicant differentiation profile.”

---

## 3.2 Calm

The product should reduce anxiety, not amplify it.
It should feel steady even when the user is stressed.

### Good

“You don’t need the perfect answer right away. Start with the strongest direction, then refine it.”

### Bad

“This is your one chance to get this exactly right.”

---

## 3.3 Premium but accessible

The brand should feel smart and polished, but not elitist or showy.

### Good

“Structured guidance for stronger applications.”

### Bad

“Elite admissions intelligence for high-performing applicant optimization.”

---

## 3.4 Honest

The product should speak with confidence, but it should not overpromise.

### Good

“This can help you find stronger story angles, but it will not replace your voice.”

### Bad

“This guarantees the strongest possible essay topic.”

---

## 3.5 Student-centered

The language should reinforce student ownership and authorship.

### Good

“Your story, shaped with better structure.”

### Bad

“Let the system write the answer for you.”

---

## 4. Brand positioning language

## 4.1 Core framing

Preferred framing:

* affordable admissions edge
* structured guidance
* authentic student story
* stronger writing with more clarity
* family visibility without chaos
* student-owned process

Avoid framing:

* hack your way in
* beat the system
* guaranteed admissions edge
* elite admissions domination
* AI does the hard part for you
* done-for-you admissions

---

## 4.2 Product description standard

Preferred description:

**The College Admissions Edge helps students build stronger college application writing with more structure, clarity, and confidence — while giving families better visibility into the process.**

Shorter version:

**Structured admissions guidance for stronger essays, supplements, and planning.**

---

## 5. Core tone by audience

## 5.1 Student-facing tone

Student-facing copy should feel:

* empowering
* clear
* non-judgmental
* grounded
* supportive without sounding childish

### Student-facing example

“You already have material worth writing about. The goal is to shape it more clearly.”

### Avoid

* talking down to the student
* sounding like a school administrator
* sounding like a motivational poster

---

## 5.2 Supporting-adult tone

Parent/supporting-adult copy should feel:

* reassuring
* summary-based
* calm
* informative
* respectful of the student’s ownership

### Supporting-adult example

“Stay informed on progress and deadlines without stepping into the student’s writing space.”

### Avoid

* language that invites micromanagement
* language that implies adults should take over authorship

---

## 5.3 Support tone

Support language should feel:

* calm
* factual
* direct
* warm without being casual
* careful around trust-sensitive issues

### Support example

“Thanks for flagging this. We’ve confirmed the issue involves your account’s premium access state, and we’re reviewing that directly.”

### Avoid

* “We apologize for any inconvenience” style filler
* false certainty
* vague reassurance without action

---

## 5.4 Admin/internal-facing operational tone

Admin and operational copy should be:

* concise
* literal
* unambiguous
* low-drama

### Example

“Deadline import completed with 18 unmatched records.”

### Avoid

* clever language
* overly branded flourish in admin surfaces

---

## 6. Writing style rules

## 6.1 Preferred sentence style

* short to medium length
* active voice
* plain English
* one clear idea at a time

### Good

“Your draft has a strong moment in the middle, but the opening still feels generic.”

### Bad

“It may be the case that the introduction is not yet fully optimized relative to the stronger content found later in the draft.”

---

## 6.2 Paragraph style

Prefer short paragraphs.
Most product copy should be scannable.
Do not create dense walls of text unless the user clearly needs a deeper explanation.

---

## 6.3 Reading level

Aim for intelligent but accessible.
The language should be understandable to:

* high school students
* parents
* counselors
  without sounding simplistic.

---

## 6.4 Contractions

Contractions are allowed and often preferred where they make the copy feel more human.

### Good

* you’re
* it’s
* don’t
* can’t

Do not overdo them in high-trust messages like billing or privacy explanations.

---

## 7. Vocabulary rules

## 7.1 Preferred words and phrases

Use language like:

* stronger direction
* clearer story
* authentic voice
* structured guidance
* next step
* progress
* refine
* revise
* grounded feedback
* school-specific
* summary view
* account link
* billing owner
* confidence state

## 7.2 Words to avoid

Avoid words like:

* leverage
* unlock your potential at scale
* optimize your narrative stack
* game-changing
* crush admissions
* dominate
* elite-only
* best-in-class AI
* magic
* guarantee
* perfect

## 7.3 Words to use carefully

These words can be used, but carefully:

* edge
* strategy
* premium
* intelligent
* guidance

They should feel grounded, not inflated.

---

## 8. Product claims rules

## 8.1 Allowed claim style

Claims should be:

* specific
* useful
* supportable
* modest enough to be trustworthy

### Good

“This can help you develop a more coherent and authentic personal statement.”

### Bad

“This guarantees the strongest possible application narrative.”

---

## 8.2 Forbidden claims

Do not say:

* guaranteed acceptance
* guaranteed stronger admissions outcome
* perfect deadline accuracy everywhere
* AI writes your final essay for you
* every school is always fully current in real time
* parents can fully review all student writing in the app

---

## 9. Emotional tone rules by context

## 9.1 Onboarding

Tone:

* encouraging
* low-pressure
* confidence-building

### Example

“You do not need the perfect answer here. Start with what feels most real.”

---

## 9.2 Snapshot

Tone:

* insightful
* clear
* validating but not flattering for its own sake

### Example

“These themes may be the strongest starting point for your application story.”

---

## 9.3 Upgrade prompts

Tone:

* confident
* practical
* value-based
* never pushy

### Example

“Turn your snapshot into a stronger personal statement, supplements plan, and school-specific writing workflow.”

### Avoid

* scarcity gimmicks
* manipulative urgency
* overhyped AI language

---

## 9.4 Billing/access issues

Tone:

* careful
* factual
* calm
* high-trust

### Example

“If payment completed but premium access still appears locked, we need to verify the billing and entitlement state directly before confirming a fix.”

---

## 9.5 Deadline and data messaging

Tone:

* transparent
* precise
* confidence-aware

### Example

“This deadline record may require direct source confirmation.”

### Avoid

* false confidence
* defensive disclaimers

---

## 9.6 Reminder emails

Tone:

* useful
* light-touch
* respectful of stress
* not nagging

### Example

“You still have progress saved here when you’re ready to continue.”

### Avoid

* guilt language
* alarmist urgency unless truly warranted

---

## 10. AI-related language rules

## 10.1 Never position AI as the hero

The student and the process are the heroes.
AI is part of the system, not the center of the brand.

### Good

“The app can help surface stronger directions and feedback.”

### Bad

“Our revolutionary AI writes admissions brilliance.”

---

## 10.2 AI output wrapper tone

When presenting AI-guided output, the surrounding language should feel:

* structured
* grounded
* non-magical
* non-absolute

### Good

“These directions look most promising based on what you shared.”

### Bad

“These are the best possible directions for your essay.”

---

## 10.3 Avoid obvious bot voice

Avoid wrapper language like:

* “Here is a response generated for you”
* “The AI has analyzed your profile and determined…”
* “As an AI…”

Prefer:

* “Here are the strongest directions we see right now.”
* “This looks like the clearest next step.”

---

## 11. Ghostwriting boundary language

This is one of the most important content rules in the product.

The app must consistently reinforce:

* the student is the author
* the app provides guidance, structure, and feedback
* the app does not ghostwrite final admissions content for submission

### Recommended language

* “Your voice, shaped more clearly.”
* “Use this as guidance, then revise in your own words.”
* “The strongest writing still sounds like you.”

### Do not use

* “Paste this directly into your application.”
* “Your final answer is ready.”
* “This writes the essay for you.”

---

## 12. Parent/supporting-adult visibility language

The product must consistently describe supporting-adult access as:

* linked
* summary-based
* visibility-oriented
* non-editorial

### Recommended language

* “Track progress and deadlines.”
* “Stay informed without stepping into the student’s writing space.”
* “See summary progress once your account is linked.”

### Do not use

* “Review every essay draft.”
* “Help edit the student’s writing inside the app.”
* “See the student’s full writing workspace.”

---

## 13. School and deadline data language

The planner should be presented as strong and useful, but honest.

### Recommended language

* “Structured school and deadline support.”
* “Some records may still require source verification.”
* “Confidence states help clarify how complete a record is.”

### Do not use

* “All deadlines are always perfectly current.”
* “Fully synced with every admissions portal.”
* “Guaranteed complete for every school at all times.”

---

## 14. Error and empty state tone

## 14.1 Error states

Error messages should be:

* calm
* specific enough to guide the user
* not technical unless necessary

### Good

“Your draft didn’t save correctly. Please try again. If this keeps happening, we’ll need to review the save state directly.”

### Bad

“Unexpected write failure: state persistence exception.”

---

## 14.2 Empty states

Empty states should feel:

* intentional
* encouraging
* directional

### Good

“You haven’t added a story yet. Start with a moment that feels important, even if it doesn’t seem polished yet.”

### Bad

“No data available.”

---

## 15. CTA rules

Calls to action should be:

* clear
* specific
* action-oriented
* not hype-heavy

### Good CTAs

* Get Your Free Edge Snapshot
* Continue Your Draft
* Review Story Directions
* Add a School
* View Progress
* Upgrade to Continue

### Weak CTAs

* Learn More
* Proceed
* Submit
* Continue Journey

### Overhyped CTAs to avoid

* Unlock Admissions Domination
* Generate Your Winning Essay Now

---

## 16. Formatting rules

## 16.1 Capitalization

Use normal title case for headers.
Do not overuse all caps.

## 16.2 Emphasis

Use bold sparingly for:

* key actions
* important clarifications
* short emphasis

Do not over-style every sentence.

## 16.3 Lists

Use lists when they improve clarity.
Keep them tight.
Do not create bloated marketing lists when a sentence is enough.

---

## 17. Support-specific style rules

Support language must:

* acknowledge the issue
* reflect what is known
* state the next step
* avoid unsupported promises

### Good

“Thanks for flagging this. We’ve confirmed that the account is still showing a free access state, so we’re now checking whether the billing and entitlement records are aligned correctly.”

### Bad

“Sorry for the inconvenience. We are looking into it.”

---

## 18. Email-specific style rules

Emails should feel:

* short
* readable on mobile
* useful quickly
* consistent with in-app voice

Each email should answer:

* why the user got it
* what changed
* what they should do next

### Good subject lines

* Your Edge Snapshot Is Ready
* You Still Have Progress Saved Here
* A Deadline Is Coming Up
* Your Premium Access Is Active

### Bad subject lines

* Action Required Immediately!!!
* Important System Notification
* Update Regarding Your Account Status

---

* revolutionary platform
* elite strategy engine
* game-changing experience

## 20. Preferred phrase list

Prefer language like:

* stronger direction
* clearer story
* structured guidance
* your next step
* authentic voice
* student-owned
* summary view
* school-specific
* progress saved
* verify directly
* supported workflow
* based on what you shared

---

## 21. Consistency rules across surfaces

The same concept should be described the same way everywhere.

### Examples

Use consistently:

* **Edge Snapshot**
* **Story Vault**
* **Personal Statement**
* **Supplements**
* **Supporting Adult** or **Parent/Supporting Adult** depending context
* **Billing Owner**
* **Linked Account**
* **Progress Summary**
* **Deadline Summary**

Do not rename the same concept unpredictably across:

* landing page
* onboarding
* support macros
* emails
* dashboard UI

---

## 22. Review and approval process

Any significant user-facing copy should be reviewed against this guide before ship.

Recommended review questions:

* Does this sound like The College Admissions Edge?
* Is it calm and clear?
* Does it overpromise?
* Does it protect the student-authorship boundary?
* Does it respect billing/privacy/data boundaries?
* Could a student and parent both understand it?

---

## 23. Non-negotiables

1. Do not overpromise what v1 can do.
2. Do not make AI sound like a ghostwriter or oracle.
3. Do not blur the student-authorship boundary.
4. Do not use inflated, elitist, or manipulative admissions language.
5. Do not allow different parts of the app to sound like different companies.

---

## 24. Final directive

This guide is the language system for v1.

If the app is going to feel premium, trustworthy, and coherent, every surface must follow the same communication logic.

The goal is not just “good copy.”
The goal is a product that sounds:

* calm under pressure
* clear when guidance matters
* honest when limits matter
* disciplined across every user touchpoint
